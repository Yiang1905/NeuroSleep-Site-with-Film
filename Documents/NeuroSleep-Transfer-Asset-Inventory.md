# NeuroSleep Transfer Asset Inventory and Due-Diligence Checklist
**Internal working document — 13 October 2026**

## Purpose
Prepare an accurate, evidence-based asset schedule for an overall project transfer. This is a working checklist, not proof of ownership or a legal opinion.

## A. Candidate assets to inventory
- Project name, logo, visual identity and brand files.
- Website source files, page assets, stylesheets, scripts and repository history.
- Concept film, original video files, exported versions, thumbnails and social cutdowns.
- Product concept descriptions and preliminary system architecture documents.
- Commercial positioning, buyer brief and market research notes.
- Presentation files and other written materials created for the project.
- Domain names, email addresses, social accounts or platform assets, only where the account is owned by the seller and the platform allows transfer.

## B. Evidence to collect for each asset
Record the exact filename/path, creation date, creator, source file, current version, current public URL (if any), ownership evidence, third-party components, open-source licenses, stock media/font licenses, AI-generated elements and any restrictions on commercial transfer.

## C. Items that must not be represented as existing without evidence
- A complete independently verifiable working hardware prototype.
- A filed patent, registered design, trademark registration or granted IP right.
- Clinical validation, diagnostic capability, regulatory approval or proven health benefit.
- A trained or validated AI model, functional app, tested sensor/actuator system or cloud service.
- A confirmed supplier, manufacturing agreement, sales revenue, paying customers or established R&D team.
- Any independently verified valuation, confirmed buyer interest or guaranteed commercial outcome.

## D. Third-party-rights review
1. Identify all stock footage, music, images, fonts, icons, templates and external code.
2. Check the exact license and whether commercial use, modification, redistribution and transfer to a buyer are permitted.
3. Check open-source dependencies and required notices.
4. Confirm whether AI tools or services impose terms affecting generated outputs or source files.
5. Do not promise transfer of platform accounts, third-party content or non-transferable service rights.

## E. Transaction guardrails
- Share the current buyer brief first; provide deeper materials only when relevant and under appropriate confidentiality terms.
- Keep the asking price as a negotiation position, not a claim of independent fair market value.
- Do not transfer ownership, repository administration, editable source packages or exclusivity before signed terms and the agreed payment milestone.
- Define deliverables, exclusions, handover assistance, payment schedule, acceptance criteria, representations and liability limits in writing.
- Obtain qualified legal advice for final IP assignment and transaction documents if the deal progresses.
