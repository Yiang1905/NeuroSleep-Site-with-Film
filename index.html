Frequently Asked Questions

Direct, honest answers to the questions that matter most.

Is there a physical prototype?

No. NeuroSleep is at concept stage: a product definition, system architecture direction, brand, website and supporting materials. There is no working hardware prototype yet.

What would a partner or licensee actually get?

The concept package: product definition, architecture direction, brand identity, website, market positioning and supporting documents — the non-technical groundwork. Ownership and usage rights are worked out according to whichever structure fits (partnership, license, or transfer). What you would not get is a de-risked, finished asset — that doesn't exist yet.

How long would it take to build a first prototype?

For a capable hardware team, a focused functional prototype (adaptive support + sensing + basic control) is realistically a 3–6 month effort, depending on resources and scope. The founder has no hardware background, so this estimate defers heavily to whoever takes that role.

Can this be licensed instead of a full partnership?

Yes, though realistically licensing fits better once a working prototype exists. Full transfer, licensing, and strategic partnership are all open for discussion — structure depends on your goals and capabilities.

Who is the ideal partner?

A technical co-founder, hardware/industrial design partner, or a small manufacturer — someone with the embedded systems, hardware, or manufacturing experience this concept doesn't have yet. Sleep/wellness industry experience is a bonus, not a requirement.

Is there a fixed price or valuation?

No. At this stage, that would be presumptuous. Terms are discussed after initial fit is confirmed, and depend heavily on what a partner brings.

Are there patents?

Not at this stage. The value is in the integrated concept, architecture, brand and commercial packaging. A buyer can pursue IP protection as part of further development.
